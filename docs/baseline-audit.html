Finding 01

These errors are systematic, not noise

Each fixture was run three times. If the errors were random sampling artifacts, they would scatter across runs. They do not.

This is the opposite of the Tier 2 grounding checks, where every failing fixture failed exactly one run in three — pure flakiness. Rules comprehension is a stable property of the prompt, which is what makes it worth measuring in an A/B at all.

The categories share a root: the advisor reads a card's narrow, conditional effect and restates it as a broad, unconditional one — usually in whatever direction supports the strategy it is already narrating.

Evidence

What that looks like in practice

Effects the card simply does not have

CARD

Feed Pellets gives animals (exchange vegetables for animals), not food.

SAID

“Priest enables early renovation for growth”

CARD

Priest grants resources if in a clay house but does not allow renovation.

SAID

“Seed Almanac and Field Caretaker enable frequent plowing”

CARD

Field Caretaker is a field tile but grants no plow actions and does not alter plow costs.

Conditions quietly dropped

SAID

CARD

Plow Hero triggers only with the first person you place in a round, and costs 1 food.

CARD

Christianity requires exactly 1 Sheep as a prerequisite, and gives opponents food.

SAID

CARD

Home Brewer turns exactly 1 grain per harvest into 3 food or 1 bonus point.

Costs described as free

SAID

“Seed Almanac … granting free plow actions after minors”

CARD

Seed Almanac requires you to pay 1 food to plow. The plow actions are not free.

SAID

“any Grain Seeds visit becomes an opportunity for a free occupation play”

CARD

Private Teacher costs 1 food per occupation played. It is not free.

Finding 03

The card text was already in the prompt

95% of card errors concern cards whose full description was supplied

Of 438 violations naming an identifiable card, 416 were about cards in the hand or already drafted — which the API enriches with full description, cost, VP and prerequisites before sending. Only 22 concerned cards the model knew by name alone.

This is not a retrieval failure, and it reshapes the remaining plan. Stage 6 (a filtered live pool) and Stage 7 (full descriptions in the static index) both address getting card text in front of the model — a problem that turns out to be already solved for the cards that actually matter.

What fails is reading it. The advisor has Plow Hero's text, containing the phrase “with the first person you place in a round”, and still writes “on each Farmland or Cultivation action”.

Round 1 responses are the cleanest because the prompt instructs brevity there — fewer claims, fewer chances to be wrong. By round 6 the advisor is describing a dozen drafted cards and their interactions, and the rate nearly doubles. The errors are worst exactly where the advice is meant to be most valuable.

Caveats

What not to read into this

Severity is not credible. The judge tagged 500 of 522 findings “major”. Spot-checking shows plenty are trivial — a missing 1 VP in a comparative aside is not a material decision error. Treat the counts as sound and the major/minor split as noise.

An earlier manual pass undercounted by roughly 8×. Judging 49 responses by hand produced 0.22 violations per response. That pass checked claims against the rules reference and repeatedly set aside card-specific claims as unverifiable — while the card text sat in the database. Seven of seven spot-checks confirmed the automated judge, not the manual one.

The judge was validated on four known-positive responses, not the full set. It beat GPT-4o decisively there (3–4 true positives against 1), but its absolute rate has not been independently confirmed at scale.

Implications

What this changes

  • The A/B has real headroom. At 1.78 violations per response there is room for the rules reference to show an effect. At the 0.22 the manual pass suggested, any change would have been swamped by run-to-run variance.
  • The rules reference targets a minority of these errors. Action-space errors, scoring mistakes and general-rules slips total roughly 100 of 522. The dominant failure — misreading a specific card's own text — is not something a rulebook fixes.
  • Stage 5 becomes the highest-value remaining work. Extracting each card's trigger, cost, cap and commitment level into structured fields converts exactly the prose conditionals that are being misread into data that cannot be paraphrased away.
  • Stages 6 and 7 look weaker than planned. Both aim to put more card text in context. Context was not the constraint.
